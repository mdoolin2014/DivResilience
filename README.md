# Investigating the impact of diversity on stability and resilience
Authors: M.L. Doolin, S.B. Weinstein, W.Z. Stephens, R. Greenhalgh, J.L. Round, M.D. Dearing

Institution: University of Utah

This is a repository for files associated with the manuscript, "Gut microbiota richness mediates mechanisms of instability during parasitism."

## Experimental approach
Mice with a phylogenetically diverse, low richness microbiota of 11 bacteria (the PhyLo11B community generated by W.Z. Stephens and J.L. Round) and typical SPF mice were challenged with infection by the small intestinal roundworm _Heligmosomoides polygyrus bakeri_. The fecal microbiota was sampled and characterized by _16S rRNA_ gene sequencing at 6 timepoints over a six-week experiment that included disturbances of worm infection and clearance. Sham-treated mice of both microbiome types were run alongside those that were experimentally infected. To examine resistance and resilience, we analyzed change between adjacent timepoints and then looked for evidence of communities recoverying toward pretreatment state after infections were cleared.  

## Significance
We contribute a new investigation in the understudied relationship between diversity with stability in host-associated microbial communities. This approach was made more similar to real-world risks of low diversity gut microbiota because mice with the synthetic gut community were housed in the same conditions as SPF mice, exposed to environmental microbes. 

## Documentation and files
This repository includes code (R and shell scripts) associated with all analyses used for data in the manuscript. We also include the phyloseq object that we produced from the cleaned and processed _16S rRNA_ sequence data and metadata files.
